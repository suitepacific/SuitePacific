Most NetSuite support providers do not publish pricing. That makes it genuinely difficult to compare options before committing to a discovery call. This benchmark pulls together what is publicly known about each pricing model, with methodology notes where estimates are used.

## Why NetSuite support pricing is hard to compare

NetSuite support is priced differently across provider types, which makes direct comparison misleading without understanding the model underneath each number.

ACS is priced as a percentage of your annual NetSuite license fee. Managed service retainers are fixed monthly fees. Break-fix consulting is billed hourly. Offshore support is also hourly but from a different cost base. Enterprise partners often blend hourly billing with minimum engagement floors.

The right comparison is cost per resolved issue, not headline rate. A $250/hour consultant who resolves an issue in one hour is cheaper than a $799/month retainer if your account generates only one issue per year. An account generating 15 issues per month flips that math quickly.

## ACS pricing

Oracle does not publish ACS pricing. Based on community-reported figures and the ACS product structure, the pattern is:

**How ACS is priced:** ACS is billed as a percentage of your annual NetSuite license fee, paid as an annual subscription. The percentage varies by tier and negotiation. Oracle negotiates ACS pricing as part of the broader licensing relationship, so the final cost depends on your license value, tier selected, and negotiation.

**Commonly reported range:** 10–20% of annual NetSuite license fee.

**Example at common license values:**

| Annual NetSuite License | ACS at 10% | ACS at 15% | ACS at 20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $30,000/year | $3,000/year | $4,500/year | $6,000/year |
| $60,000/year | $6,000/year | $9,000/year | $12,000/year |
| $100,000/year | $10,000/year | $15,000/year | $20,000/year |
| $150,000/year | $15,000/year | $22,500/year | $30,000/year |

These are estimates based on structure rather than published rates. Your actual ACS cost is in your renewal quote.

**ACS tier structure (published by Oracle):**

| Tier | Named hours | Typical focus |
|---|---|---|
| Advise | Shared pool | New or growing accounts |
| Monitor | ~36 hrs / quarter | Platform best practices, proactive reviews |
| Optimize | ~20 hrs / month | Feature adoption, optimization guidance |
| Architect | ~40 hrs / month | Enterprise, multi-subsidiary |

**What ACS covers and what it does not:**

ACS covers best-practice guidance, proactive account reviews, and faster escalation of platform issues to Oracle engineering. Per Oracle's service descriptions, ACS does not cover custom SuiteScript, third-party integrations, implementation-partner configurations, or anything built on top of the standard NetSuite platform. Custom support work on those layers requires the team that built them.

## Managed service retainer pricing

Managed service retainers from boutique post-go-live firms are fixed monthly fees covering development, administration, break-fix, and upgrade preparation. Unlike ACS, most managed service providers do not publish pricing.

**Estimated range from other providers:**

Based on market conversations and publicly available information where available, boutique managed service firms typically range from $1,000–$5,000 per month depending on account complexity and hours included. Most providers do not publish rates; estimates vary by firm size, location, and engagement scope.

**What managed service retainers typically cover:**

Covered: SuiteScript development, SuiteFlow workflow changes, saved search development, integration maintenance, administration (roles, fields, configuration), break-fix on all of the above, and upgrade preparation before each twice-yearly NetSuite release.

Not covered in most retainers: net-new module implementations of material scope, new integrations built from scratch (typically scoped separately), and accounting structure redesigns.

## Break-fix and hourly consulting pricing

Hourly consulting is the default model for project-based work and one-off fixes. The range is wide depending on the firm's size, location, and specialization.

| Provider type | Estimated hourly rate | Typical minimum |
|---|---|---|
| US boutique firm | $150–$250/hr | Project minimum varies |
| US enterprise partner | $200–$350/hr | Often $5,000+ minimum engagement |
| Offshore firm (India/Philippines) | $40–$90/hr | Varies |
| Freelance consultant (US) | $100–$200/hr | None typically |

These are market estimates. Published rates for NetSuite consulting are rare.

**When break-fix makes sense:** If your account is stable and generates fewer than 2–3 issues per quarter, break-fix or a small project budget is often more cost-effective than a monthly retainer. Once issues become more frequent or require ongoing context, a retainer model typically becomes more efficient.

## What makes each model more or less expensive per resolved issue

The headline price is less important than what drives actual cost in practice.

**ACS:** The cost-per-issue depends on what percentage of your issues fall within ACS scope. If 70% of your support needs involve customizations, integrations, or things the implementation partner built, a significant portion of your ACS spend goes toward work ACS cannot do. The effective cost of in-scope work is higher than the total ACS fee implies.

**Managed retainer:** Cost-per-issue depends on volume. At $799/month with 15 issues resolved, the effective rate per resolved issue is low. At $799/month with 1 issue resolved, it is high. Retainer pricing rewards accounts with consistent support volume.

**Break-fix hourly:** Cost scales directly with issue volume and complexity. Unpredictable. Works well for low-volume accounts or large one-time projects.

## Total cost of support: a worked example

Consider an account with a $60,000 annual NetSuite license generating an average of 8 support requests per month, split roughly:
- 3 standard platform or configuration questions
- 3 SuiteScript or workflow issues
- 2 integration troubleshooting requests

Under ACS at 15% of license: approximately $9,000/year ($750/month). Platform and configuration questions are in scope; SuiteScript, workflow, and integration issues require the implementation partner or another firm. The three platform requests per month are covered; the five customization and integration requests are not, and require separate billing.

Under a managed retainer at $1,499/month: $17,988/year. All eight request types are covered within the retainer. No separate billing for the SuiteScript, workflow, or integration requests.

The managed retainer costs more in this example. Whether it represents better value depends on what the implementation partner or break-fix consultant charges for the five requests per month that ACS does not cover.

## Key questions when evaluating support pricing

1. What percentage of your actual support requests involve customizations, integrations, or things your implementation partner built? That percentage determines how much of an ACS subscription covers your real needs.
2. Does the provider publish pricing, or do you need a discovery call to get a number? Providers that publish pricing are easier to evaluate without a sales process.
3. What is the contract structure? ACS requires an annual upfront commitment. Most managed service providers offer month-to-month after a short initial period.
4. What is the response commitment on production-down issues? Both ACS and managed service firms typically commit to same-day or next-business-day responses, but the routing model differs.
