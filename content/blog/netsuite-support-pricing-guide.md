## What Are the Four NetSuite Support Service Models?

### 1. Monthly retainer (most common for ongoing support)

A retainer is a fixed monthly fee covering an agreed block of hours. The business gets a dedicated resource available each month; the consultant gets predictable revenue and deep context into the account over time.

**Typical structure:** A fixed fee per month covering a defined number of hours. Additional hours beyond the block are billed at an agreed overage rate. Most retainers are month-to-month with no minimum commitment, though some firms require 3 to 6 month minimums.

**Typical price range:**
- Small business focused (10-50 users, administration-heavy): $1,500 to $3,000 per month
- Mid-market (50-200 users, mix of administration and development): $3,000 to $6,000 per month
- Complex accounts (multi-subsidiary, heavy customization, significant development needs): $5,000 to $10,000+ per month

**What drives cost within the retainer range:**
- Hours included in the block (more hours, higher fee)
- Mix of administration versus development work (development rates are higher)
- Account complexity (more subsidiaries, more customizations, more integration maintenance)
- Response time expectations (same-day versus next-day response)
- Provider type (boutique versus large practice versus Oracle ACS, discussed below)

### 2. Project-based or time-and-materials

For defined-scope work (a health check, an account optimization engagement, a specific SuiteScript customization, or an integration build), project pricing is based on estimated hours at an hourly rate.

**Typical hourly rates:**
- Boutique firms with direct developer access: $150 to $225 per hour
- Mid-size consulting practices: $175 to $275 per hour
- Large NetSuite partners (Big-4 adjacent, named accounts): $225 to $375 per hour
- Freelancers (individual contractors): $100 to $175 per hour

**What drives rate differences within each tier:**
- Certification level (SuiteCloud Developer II commands a premium over uncertified developers)
- Seniority (senior developers with 5+ years of NetSuite experience versus juniors)
- Access model (direct developer versus account manager relay)
- Geographic location (US-based versus offshore teams operating during US business hours)
- Firm overhead (larger firms carry more overhead, which is built into rates)

**Project scope examples and typical ranges:**
- NetSuite health check: $2,500 to $6,000 depending on account complexity
- Account optimization engagement: $5,000 to $15,000 depending on scope of cleanup
- Single SuiteScript customization (user event, moderate complexity): $2,000 to $5,000
- Integration with a third-party platform: $5,000 to $20,000+ depending on complexity
- Implementation rescue: $8,000 to $40,000+ depending on how much needs to be rebuilt

### 3. Oracle Advanced Customer Support (ACS)

Oracle's own post-go-live support program. Typically sold in three tiers:

- **ACS Basic/Standard:** Technical support access plus some advisory hours, starting around $2,500 to $5,000 per month.
- **ACS Managed Services:** Broader scope including configuration work, typically $5,000 to $15,000+ per month.
- **ACS Strategic:** Dedicated resources for large enterprise accounts, custom pricing.

**The important caveats about ACS:**
The scope of what ACS covers is defined by Oracle's service catalog, not by what your account needs. Work outside that catalog is not covered. ACS resources are shared pools, which means response times and continuity vary. The program works well for companies that need a stable, well-defined support contract; it works less well for companies that need agile access to a developer who understands their specific customizations.

### 4. Break-fix or on-demand (no retainer)

Some firms will take project calls without an ongoing retainer, billing hourly as work comes in. This is common for one-off requests: a broken script, a single saved search, a role change.

The trade-off is context: an on-demand consultant has no institutional knowledge of your account and charges time to understand the situation before fixing it. Over time, on-demand support from multiple sources accumulates inconsistent decisions in the account. For routine ongoing needs, a retainer relationship is almost always more cost-effective than on-demand billing once you factor in context overhead.

## What Should You Watch For in a NetSuite Support Proposal?

**Vague hours:** A retainer described as "up to X hours per month" or "a block of support hours" should specify exactly what work is in scope. Some firms include development time in their retainer scope; others include only administration and bill development separately at a different rate.

**Offshore delivery at US-rate pricing:** Some large firms price retainers at US rates but deliver work through offshore teams operating US hours. This is legal and sometimes appropriate, but the delivery model affects turnaround time, communication, and accountability. Ask directly who will be doing the work.

**Minimum commitments that exceed your needs:** A firm requiring a 12-month minimum retainer for a 10-user account with modest admin needs is pricing for their own convenience, not yours. Month-to-month is standard for boutique and mid-market NetSuite support firms.

**Scope creep toward implementation pricing:** Some support firms quote a retainer rate, then bill development work at a separate higher rate that was not disclosed upfront. The retainer covers administration; everything else is extra. Understand the scope boundary before signing.

**The account manager relay:** When the person you speak with during sales is not the person who will do the work, and when requests go through a ticket system rather than directly to the developer, context is lost at every step. This is typical of larger firms and some managed service providers. The resulting overhead is built into the rate.

## How Do You Get the Right Price for Your NetSuite Support Needs?

The range above is wide because NetSuite support needs vary enormously. A 15-user account that went live 18 months ago and runs smoothly has fundamentally different needs from a 200-user multi-subsidiary account with active integrations and a developer-heavy customization backlog.

Before evaluating a proposal:

1. **Estimate your monthly request volume.** How many configuration requests, user changes, and development asks do you typically generate in a month? If you don't have a clear sense, track informally for 30 days before committing to a retainer.

2. **Identify the mix of work.** Administration-heavy accounts (roles, user management, saved searches, form configuration) are less expensive to support than development-heavy accounts (scripts, integrations, workflow automation). The rate difference is meaningful.

3. **Know your critical path.** If a broken script affecting AP needs to be fixed today, not next week, that response time expectation should be part of the retainer agreement explicitly, not assumed.
