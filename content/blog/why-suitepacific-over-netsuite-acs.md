NetSuite ACS is built around the standard platform. SuitePacific is built around your account. That distinction shapes everything: what gets covered, how you access support, and what it costs.

This is a direct comparison. We are not neutral. We believe SuitePacific is the right choice for the majority of SMBs that are evaluating their ACS renewal. We also believe in being specific about where ACS has a genuine structural advantage, because accounts that need that advantage should know it before they switch.

## What does SuitePacific cover that ACS does not?

The scope difference is not marginal. ACS covers Oracle's standard platform. Everything built on top of that platform, or connected to it, falls outside ACS scope at every tier from Advise through Architect.

SuitePacific covers:

**SuiteScript 2.x development and debugging.** User Event Scripts, Scheduled Scripts, Map/Reduce Scripts, Suitelets, RESTlets, and Client Scripts. This includes scripts written during your original implementation that ACS will not touch, plus new development as your account evolves.

**Third-party integration maintenance.** Celigo flows, Shopify connectors, Salesforce syncs, HubSpot integrations, REST and SOAP API connections, EDI, and 3PL integrations. When a sync fails or records stop flowing, SuitePacific diagnoses the full stack, not just the NetSuite side of the connection.

**Custom workflow logic.** Standard SuiteFlow is covered by ACS. Complex multi-condition workflows, scheduled workflows, workflows that trigger scripts, and custom configurations built by your implementation partner are not. SuitePacific covers all of it.

**Saved searches, formulas, and reporting.** Standard saved search behavior is within ACS scope. Complex formula columns, joined searches, SuiteAnalytics Workbook configurations, and implementation-specific reporting structures are not. SuitePacific covers the full reporting layer.

**Advanced PDF and FreeMarker templates.** Transaction templates, email templates, and FreeMarker logic for invoices, purchase orders, packing slips, and custom documents are out of ACS scope. These are in scope for SuitePacific.

**NetSuite administration.** Role configuration, permission management, user provisioning, subsidiary setup, and account administration are covered. ACS covers configuration guidance; SuitePacific handles the actual configuration.

## How is the support model different?

ACS operates as a shared resource pool. When you submit a case, it enters a queue. The consultant who handles it may not have worked on your account before. They do not carry forward context from previous cases. Each case starts from zero.

SuitePacific works as a retained engagement. One consultant handles your account. By the second month, that consultant knows your scripts, your integrations, your recurring issues, and your environment. By the sixth month, the context is deep enough that complex issues get resolved faster because the account history is already understood.

The response model is also different. ACS routes cases through a tier structure. SuitePacific provides direct contact to the consultant doing the work. When something breaks in production, you reach the person who will fix it.

## Where does ACS have a genuine advantage?

One area: Oracle-internal escalation.

ACS has a direct path to Oracle engineering for confirmed platform defects. When a genuine platform bug is identified, ACS can file it internally with Oracle's development team. This is not a bureaucratic advantage; it is a structural one that comes from ACS being an Oracle product.

SuitePacific does not have Oracle-internal escalation access. We identify platform bugs, document them thoroughly, and guide you through submitting them to NetSuite support via the standard support channel. That process works, but it does not carry the same priority path as an ACS internal filing.

For accounts that regularly encounter genuine platform defects and rely on that escalation path, this matters. For accounts where platform escalation is rare or has never been needed, it is not a meaningful factor in the decision.

## What accounts are the best fit for SuitePacific?

SMBs that have been live on NetSuite for at least six months and have accumulated a customization layer. Specifically:

- Accounts running SuiteScript that was built during implementation and needs ongoing maintenance
- Accounts using Celigo, Shopify, Salesforce, or other third-party integrations that surface NetSuite-side issues
- Accounts where the implementation partner has rolled off and there is no internal technical resource for NetSuite
- Accounts paying for ACS and separately paying a consultant for customization work because ACS cannot handle it
- Accounts approaching an ACS renewal and questioning whether the scope matches what they actually use

The account does not need to be particularly large. The determining factor is the presence of a customization layer that requires ongoing technical attention, not the account size.

## What accounts might be a better fit for ACS or a hybrid arrangement?

Accounts that primarily need functional guidance on standard NetSuite features and whose support requests rarely involve SuiteScript or integrations are well-served by ACS.

Accounts that have had repeated genuine platform defects, where Oracle-internal escalation delivered a resolution that would not have come through standard support, benefit from keeping ACS specifically for that escalation path. In those situations, a hybrid arrangement works: ACS for Oracle escalation, SuitePacific for the technical layer. Running both simultaneously is permitted by Oracle and eliminates the scope gap entirely.

## How do you get started with SuitePacific?

The onboarding process takes two to four weeks. Week one covers the agreement and provisioning access at the appropriate NetSuite role level. Weeks two through four cover the consultant's review of your scripts, integrations, workflows, and documented issues directly in the account. No documentation package is required from your team.

Most accounts start while ACS is still active and run a parallel overlap period. New requests route to SuitePacific; platform escalation remains with ACS during the overlap. At ACS renewal, the contract is not renewed.

The three-month minimum begins at the start of the engagement, not at the end of the parallel period.

## Frequently asked questions about SuitePacific vs. NetSuite ACS

**Is SuitePacific certified at the same level as ACS resources?**
Yes. Oracle NetSuite Certified SuiteCloud Developer II and Oracle NetSuite Certified Administrator Professional. These are the same credential standards that ACS-affiliated resources hold.

**Can SuitePacific handle a NetSuite upgrade?**
Yes. Upgrade impact testing, sandbox validation, and post-upgrade issue resolution are part of the retainer scope. The biannual NetSuite release cycle (2026.1 in February, 2026.2 in August) is a standard part of ongoing account management.

**What if I have both SuiteScript and functional questions?**
Both are covered. SuitePacific handles technical work (SuiteScript, integrations, workflows) and functional guidance (feature configuration, process questions, best practice recommendations). The scope is not limited to development.

**How does the month-to-month model work after the three-month minimum?**
After three months, the engagement continues on a rolling monthly basis with 30 days notice to cancel. There is no annual lock-in after the initial minimum.

**Can SuitePacific take over a codebase that was built by another firm?**
Yes. This is a common starting point. The onboarding review covers existing scripts, integrations, and workflows written by a previous partner. The consultant documents the codebase and becomes the ongoing maintainer.
