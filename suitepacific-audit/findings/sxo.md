# SXO Audit: SuitePacific.com

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Analyst scope:** Homepage, /hire-netsuite-developer, /netsuite-consulting-services, /netsuite-post-go-live-support, /netsuite-suitescript-development, /netsuite-workflow-automation, /netsuite-administrator-support, /netsuite-admin-support-small-business, /case-studies, Blog (39 posts), Resources (30 pages)
**Target audience:** NetSuite admins, finance/ops managers, IT directors at 50-500 employee companies
**SXO Gap Score: 50 / 100**

---

## Executive Summary

SuitePacific has strong content depth and genuine E-E-A-T signals: Oracle-certified credentials, sandbox-first methodology, specific technical framing across service pages, and a 39-post blog that covers real practitioner questions. The structural gap is two-layered.

First, conversion architecture fails across four of five service pages: the primary CTA exits to /contact, mid-page forms are absent, and social proof is gated behind the hire page alone. Second, the content strategy has generated a cannibalization problem: blog posts now compete against service pages for the same commercial queries, splitting Google's topical authority signal. These two issues explain most of the gap between content quality and conversion performance.

The homepage title targets "post-go-live support" but the page is a multi-service hub. The dedicated /netsuite-post-go-live-support page is the correct commercial asset for that query and should be treated as the primary page for that cluster, not the homepage.

## Primary Finding: Page-Type and Intent Analysis

### Finding 1: Homepage title tag conflicts with dedicated service page

**Severity: HIGH**

The homepage title tag claims "Post-Go-Live NetSuite Support & Optimization" while a dedicated /netsuite-post-go-live-support page exists. Google must choose between two pages from the same domain for this query. The homepage will likely cannibalize the dedicated page because of its domain authority and inbound link concentration.

**Fix:** Revise the homepage title to a brand-centric statement ("SuitePacific: NetSuite Support and Development Services") and ensure /netsuite-post-go-live-support carries the keyword-specific title and receives internal link support from the homepage's service section card.

---

### Finding 2: "netsuite consulting services" page faces implementation-SERP mismatch

**Severity: HIGH**

Page type is correct (service landing page) but topical alignment with the SERP is poor. Searchers using "netsuite consulting services" are predominantly pre-implementation or mid-implementation. SuitePacific's explicit "we do not do implementations" framing is a relevance signal reduction.

**Fix (two-track):**
- Restructure the /netsuite-consulting-services opening to lead with outcomes and capabilities before the scope qualifier. Move "what we do not do" to the FAQ.
- Target long-tail variants with better natural alignment: "netsuite managed services consulting," "netsuite post-implementation support services," "outsourced netsuite team."

---

### Finding 3: Blog-to-service page cannibalization (HIGH RISK)

**Severity: HIGH**

The 39-post blog contains multiple posts that target the same queries as service pages. Google must decide which page to rank. Without explicit canonicalization signals or strong internal linking hierarchies, the blog post may outrank the service page because blog content is often fresher and earns more backlinks from informational queries.

High-risk overlaps identified:

| Service Page | Competing Blog Post(s) |
|---|---|
| /netsuite-post-go-live-support | "NetSuite Post-Go-Live Checklist: What to Prioritize in Your First 90 Days" and "How to Evaluate a NetSuite Post-Go-Live Support Partner" and "8 Signs Your NetSuite Support Isn't Working" |
| /netsuite-workflow-automation | "5 Common NetSuite Workflow Automation Mistakes" and "How to Build an Approval Workflow in NetSuite SuiteFlow" and "NetSuite Workflow vs SuiteScript: Which to Use and When" |
| /netsuite-suitescript-development | "SuiteScript Best Practices: Customizations That Survive the Next Upgrade" and "NetSuite Map/Reduce Scripts: A Complete Guide" and "NetSuite User Event Scripts vs Client Scripts" |
| /netsuite-account-optimization | "NetSuite Account Optimization: What to Audit and Fix on a Live Account" and "Why Your NetSuite Account Feels Slow" |
| /netsuite-administrator-support | "NetSuite Month-End Close Checklist" and "NetSuite Saved Search Examples for Finance and Operations Teams" |

**Fix:**
- Add explicit `<link rel="canonical">` on each blog post pointing to itself (default, confirms it as the canonical), then add strong contextual internal links FROM each blog post TO the relevant service page with anchor text like "see our NetSuite workflow automation service" or "our SuiteScript development team handles this."
- Use the service page as the anchor for the cluster: it should link TO the blog posts (as supporting content), and the blog posts should link BACK to the service page (as the conversion destination). This creates a hub-and-spoke signal that tells Google which page carries the commercial intent.
- Consider adding a "related service" module to the bottom of each blog post (above the LeadFormLight) that presents one service card with a brief description and CTA.

### Finding 4: CRITICAL - Primary CTA exits page on all service pages

**Severity: CRITICAL**

Every service page places "Book a Free Consultation" buttons that navigate to /contact, removing the visitor from the page where they built context and intent. Four of five service pages have only a single form instance, placed after the FAQ section. Most B2B visitors exit before reaching 50% scroll depth.

**Evidence:**
- /hire-netsuite-developer: 2 LeadForms (mid-page and bottom) - only compliant page
- /netsuite-post-go-live-support: 1 LeadForm at bottom, all buttons exit to /contact
- /netsuite-consulting-services: 1 LeadForm at bottom, all buttons exit to /contact
- /netsuite-suitescript-development: 1 LeadForm at bottom, all buttons exit to /contact
- /netsuite-admin-support-small-business: 1 LeadForm at bottom, all buttons exit to /contact

**Fix:**
- Change Button `href="/contact"` to `href="#contact"` on all service pages
- Ensure each service page has `id="contact"` on its LeadForm wrapper
- Add a mid-page LeadForm to the four non-compliant service pages, positioned after the service coverage list (section 2 or 3 of each page), using the same pattern as /hire-netsuite-developer

### Finding 5: Social proof absent from four of five service pages

**Severity: HIGH**

The hire page includes three case study cards with concrete scenario descriptions. None of the other four primary service pages include any case study, outcome statement, or named work example. "Why SuitePacific" trust cards (Oracle-Certified, Fast Turnaround, Direct Access) present self-asserted claims with no external validation.

**Fix:**
- Add a 2-card case study strip to each service page using existing case study content
- Map: /netsuite-post-go-live-support: Invoice Processing Automation + Operational Reporting
- Map: /netsuite-suitescript-development: Vendor Quotation Management + Sales Order Workflow
- Map: /netsuite-workflow-automation: Sales Order Approval Workflow + Invoice Processing
- Map: /netsuite-admin-support-small-business: any two appropriate case studies
- Rewrite case study cards to include at least one numeric outcome each

### Finding 6: No pricing context on any service page

**Severity: HIGH**

No service page discloses a price range. The hire page lists three engagement models (hourly, fixed-price, retainer) without rates. Competing fractional admin pages explicitly state "$3,000 to $10,000/month" and lead with cost-vs-hiring math. CFOs and Controllers cannot self-qualify without a price signal.

**Fix:**
- Add a one-paragraph pricing context section to each service page: "Retainer engagements typically start at [X] hours per month and are scoped based on your account's complexity. We discuss pricing in the free consultation call."
- The admin-support-small-business page already references "$80k or more per year" as the cost of a full-time admin. Add the corresponding monthly retainer context as a range.
- No need to publish exact rates. A range or minimum is sufficient to qualify budget-conscious buyers.

### Finding 7: Mobile navigation requires 3-4 taps to reach a service page

**Severity: MEDIUM**

The Services nav item links to /#services (homepage anchor), not to a services index or dropdown. On mobile: user opens menu, taps Services, navigates to homepage, scrolls to services section, taps a service card. This is a 4-step path. Competing sites use a Services dropdown directly from the nav.

**Fix:**
- Convert the Services nav item to a dropdown (same pattern as Products) listing the 5-6 core service pages directly
- Suggested entries: Hire a NetSuite Developer, Post-Go-Live Support, NetSuite Consulting, SuiteScript Development, Workflow Automation, Admin Support
- This reduces mobile path to 2 taps (open menu, tap service name) and improves Googlebot crawl depth

### Finding 8: admin-support vs admin-support-small-business - near-duplicate overlap

**Severity: MEDIUM**

/netsuite-administrator-support and /netsuite-admin-support-small-business cover identical services (user management, roles, custom fields, saved searches, period management, data imports) with near-identical "Why SuitePacific" cards. Both pages target similar queries. Google may not clearly differentiate which page to rank for which intent.

**Fix:**
- Clearly differentiate scope: /netsuite-administrator-support should target mid-market companies with more complex setups (multiple subsidiaries, advanced modules); /netsuite-admin-support-small-business targets owner-operators and companies of 5-50 employees
- Add size/complexity signals to each page's opening paragraph and FAQ
- Add a cross-link between the two pages with explicit framing ("For smaller teams: Admin Support for Small Business / For growing enterprises: Administrator Support")

---

### Finding 9: Case studies lack numeric outcomes

**Severity: HIGH (bottom-of-funnel impact)**

All 6 case studies use qualitative language ("now runs overnight," "became visible at a glance," "complete audit trail"). No case study specifies time saved, headcount avoided, cost reduced, or error rate cut. For IT Directors and CFOs at the decision stage, proof requires numbers.

**Fix:**
- Revisit each case study and add at minimum one metric. Even conservative approximations are more credible than none: "reduced from 4 hours of manual entry to a 20-minute overnight run," "eliminated the weekly export step that previously took 2-3 hours per team member."
- Add client industry and company size to each case study, even if anonymized ("a 90-person distribution company").
- Add a "Challenge / Solution / Outcome" three-section structure to make scanning faster.

---

### Finding 10: Schema coverage is thin across service pages

**Severity: MEDIUM**

All service pages have BreadcrumbJsonLd and FaqJsonLd. No Service schema (schema.org/Service), no Organization schema propagated from homepage, no AggregateRating schema anywhere on the site.

**Fix:**
- Add ServiceJsonLd to each service page (name, description, provider, serviceType, areaServed, url)
- Evaluate adding AggregateRating only if authentic client reviews are collected. A fabricated rating is a quality guideline violation.

## User Journey Assessment

### Can a visitor find pricing/contact from any page?

Contact: Partially yes. The "Book a Free Consultation" button is present on every page via the nav CTA and service page sections. However, clicking it exits the current page, which is a friction point.

Pricing: No. No service page, resource page, or FAQ discloses a price range or starting rate. A visitor who arrives from a SERP result and wants to know whether SuitePacific is within their budget must submit a form to find out. This gatekeeping is common in B2B consulting but reduces conversion from budget-conscious mid-market buyers.

Recommended journey improvement: Add a "How pricing works" anchor link in the site footer and a consistent pricing FAQ across all service pages. The FAQ can contain three entries: "What does a retainer cost?", "Do you charge by the hour?", "Do I need a long-term contract?" All three of these questions appear in Google's PAA results for NetSuite consulting queries.

## Limitations

- SERP positions were observed at a single point in time from a US-based general session. Results vary by geolocation, personalization, and time of day. Positions were not verified via GSC impression data.
- Conversion rate data (form submission rates, bounce rates by page, scroll depth) is unavailable. Gap scores are structural inferences, not A/B test outcomes.
- Mobile rendering was assessed via nav structure analysis and page content review, not a physical device test. Tap-target sizes and actual render performance on small screens were not verified.
- Case study claim about "no numeric outcomes" was assessed from the case studies hub page content. Individual case study detail pages may contain metrics not visible on the hub.
- Schema assessment is based on what was detectable in rendered page HTML via WebFetch. Some schema types (JSON-LD blocks injected late by JavaScript) may not have been captured.
- No access to Google Search Console data: which pages receive impressions for which queries, what click-through rates are, and whether any pages are already flagged for cannibalization, are all unknown.
- Review signals (Clutch, G2, testimonials with named clients) were not evaluated in scope per site policy on identity disclosure.
